4c · Confidence tagging · sample comparison cell

Every pricing data point carries one of three tags. unavailable is the Pydantic schema default — an unsourced cell is never presented as confirmed. That decision came out of a failed eval, not the original design.

CRITERION CISCO CATALYST 9300 ARISTA CCS-720XP
cycle row 3:

confirmed — vendor-direct pricing page, official datasheet, or Tier-1 reseller (CDW, Insight, SHI). Verifiable URL included.

estimated — indirect source, secondary citation, or industry-typical range. Surfaced honestly to the user, never disguised as a quote.

unavailable — web search returned nothing usable. The cell ships with a recommendation to contact the vendor directly. The model never fills the gap with an invented number.

§ 05 · The system prompt anchors

Five hard-stop rules. Injected every turn.

The Advisor's system prompt isn't a one-time setup. It's reinjected on every turn so it can't drift mid-conversation.

Never present unverified pricing as confirmed. The confidence indicator is always surfaced.

Never provide CLI commands or device configuration syntax. Configuration is not this product's lane.

Never recommend a single vendor without presenting at least one alternative or framing tradeoffs.

Always include the planning-only disclaimer on every PDF export. It is not optional.

Never generate financial projections beyond five years. Outside a reasonable planning horizon.

§ 06 · The advisor in action

What governed AI sounds like in practice.

Illustrative exchange — Advisor output is non-deterministic, so your run will vary in wording. What's invariant: CapEx/OpEx framing, refusal to fabricate a price, and explicit handoff to the Comparison view.

NETWORK ENGINEER

Given a 600-endpoint access-layer refresh across 3 sites with a $300K five-year ceiling, what device classes should I consider and what's the rough TCO shape I should plan around? I need to defend this to a CFO.

NETPLANNER ADVISOR · sonnet-4-6

For 600 wired endpoints across three sites, you're squarely in enterprise access-layer fixed switching territory. The two device classes worth modeling are full-stack enterprise (Cisco Catalyst 9300 / Juniper EX4400) and lean enterprise (Arista CCS-720XP / Aruba CX 6300). They diverge on licensing model and ecosystem maturity more than on raw throughput.

On TCO shape, plan for a front-loaded CapEx in Year 1 — hardware plus first-year support — followed by a flat OpEx tail of recurring software licensing across Years 2–5. For a roughly 14-switch deployment, a $300K ceiling is achievable with the lean-enterprise class and tight with the full-stack class at higher software feature levels.

I won't quote specific list prices here — they vary by reseller, contract size, and any active deal registration on file. Pull the two vendors into the Comparison view and I'll surface what the research agent can confirm versus estimate.
